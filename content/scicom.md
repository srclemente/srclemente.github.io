## Teaching 
I am passionate about teaching and have taken every opportunity to balance science education with the research responsibilities of my doctoral thesis and postdoc. I'm happy to have had several chances to teach in the college classroom, from undergraduate freshmen in first-year seminars to final-semester students in the labs of a graduate-level animal behavior course. Below, you can find selections of lectures, activities, notes, and some exemplary student work from the courses I've taught.

* ***FYS 191 Syllabus*** // A [syllabus crafted for a first-year seminar](../files/fys_191_fa23_syllabus.pdf), following principles of inclusive course design & syllabus writing.

* ***Lab 7 Reading Guides*** // For an advanced animal behavior course, I led a "jigsaw" style discussion where students selected one paper from a series, read it independently, discussed it with folks who read the same paper, and then shared a summary with classmates who selected other papers. To best facilitate discussion, [I created this guide](../files/lab_7_reading_guides.pdf)  to help students navigate each paper while they read independently, or as they discussed in class. 

* ***Parenting in the Animal Kingdom Lesson*** // This is a lesson I created for an introductory animal behavior course focusing on parenting behaviors in the animal kingdom - why animals parent, and the types of ways animals parent. For the latter theme, I wanted to cover the many examples of animal parenting behavior in an engaging and a time-sensitive manner, so I asked students to do a *Bluff the Listener* activity in the style of the NPR comedy show *Wait, Wait Don't Tell Me!*. 

  * [ACTIVITY WORKSHEET](../files/wwdtm_activity.pdf) // Try  it yourself!

  * [LECTURE & ANNOTATED NOTES](../files/week_8_n.pdf) // The lecture slides, annotated. Black boxes depict topics we discussed verbally in class. Generally, I make the un-annotated version available to students before class, and an annotated version after.

* ***BIO 550 Final Projects*** // As the lab instructor for an advanced animal behavior course, I was tasked to walk students through a final project where they designed their own manipulative experiments or observations of lab-reared or wild animals. The project was an involved process where groups met one-on-one with me to develop a project design, experimental workflow, and statistical approach.

  Below, I include exemplary student final projects, ensuring that I cover a breadth of work that best represents the general student experience. I try to include projects that  represent statistically and non-statistically significant results, wild and lab-reared animal subjects, and experiences where students crafted their own project from scratch or adapted an aspect of their concurrent extracurricular research.

## Engagement and Science Communication 
I believe that community outreach is an essential part of making my research accessible. In my career as a scientist, I am honored to have had the opportunity to engage with a wide variety of audiences, whether it is from the front of classroom or from within a tent at a county fair.

* **The Donne' Såli Restoration Project** // My current postdoctoral work centers around the seed dispersal dynamics of the pepper *Capsicum frutescens* in the Mariana Islands,  it is known as *donne' såli* and is a culturally valuable plant that is a quintessential part of local cuisine. [Check out our efforts to create a robust population of donne' såli on the island of Guåhan, where it is under threat.](https://srclemente.github.io/donne)

* **Nature Club with Girls, Inc. of the Valley** //  I worked with other UMass Amherst Graduate Students to run an afterschool program for elementary school girls with modules focusing on environmental science and ecology. Together with the staff at Girls, Inc. and a professor specializing in inclusive pedagogy, we took a summer class on inclusion in science pedagogy and developed activities to fill the once-weekly 9-week program. This year (2026) is my third year participating with Nature Club!

  Along with supporting other instructors, I lead a lesson module focused on introducing participants to the importance of data and statistics in science, and included an activity that used Skittles candy to demonstrate mean, median, and mode. Here is my [lesson plan](../files/lesson7.pdf) and an example of [activity sheet](../files/skittles.pdf) I developed for this module!

* **Science Cafe** // From 2020-2023, I was part of the organizing team for Science Cafe (now [Science Stories](https://sci-stories.org/)), an outreach organization that organized interview-style talks between faculty for the general public. I was the host of [one such Science Cafe interview with fellow chemical ecologist Dr. Sibongile Mafu](https://sci-stories.org/dr-sibongile-mafu/)!  

* **3-Minute Thesis Competition** // I was a finalist in the annual UMass Amherst 3-Minute Thesis in 2022 and 2024. As with other 3MT competitions held in academic instutions around the world, participants are asked to summarize their thesis work in under three minutes for a general audience. You can watch [my 2022 talk](https://youtu.be/9nmXPJibK9A) or my [2024 talk, which won 2nd Place and the People's Choice Award!](https://youtu.be/QcWqF-ujDEY?si=q9TnNmmFh16RqjSZ)
