## Protecting a Cultural Treasure of the Mariana Islands

Introduced species have wreaked havoc on the native flora and fauna of the Mariana Islands. This is especially visible on Guåhan (Guam), where the brown tree snake (*Boiga irregularis*) has decimated the native avian community and resulted in the extirpation and extinction of several endemic bird species. The removal of forest birds has downstream effects on native fauna that rely on avian seed dispersal, further complicated by large populations of introduced ungulates such as *binådu* (deer, *Rusa marianna*) and *babui* (wild pigs, *Sus scrofa*). 

*Donne' såli* is a culturally valuable plant that is quintessential to CHamoru cuisine. On Guåhan, wild populations of *donne' såli*, were once widespread.  **Today, in jungles devoid of birdsong, donne' såli is rarely found. Our mission at the Donne' Såli Restoration Project is to reestablish  wild donne' såli populations for the people of Guåhan.**

## What Is Donne' Såli?

*Donne' såli* (*donni såli* in the Northern Marianas Islands) is the CHamoru name for *Capsicum frutescens*,  commonly known as tabasco pepper or birdseye pepper. Native to South America, its bright, red, pungent fruits are thought to be an adaptation to attract birds which disperse the plant's seeds. *Donne' såli* was introduced to the Marianas as part of the Spanish galleon trade as [early as the late 17th century](https://doi.org/10.1663/0013-0001(2005)059[0018:CFLISA]2.0.CO;2), and native birds filled the niche as the plant's seed dispersers. The plant's CHamoru name (recorded as early as 1905 in Safford's *Useful Plants of Guam*) divulges a long-held understanding of this relationship: *donne'* refers to any *Capsicum* pepper, while *såli* refers to the Mariana starlings (*Aplonis opaca*) that are commonly seen picking the plant's fruit. [Camera observations](https://doi.org/10.1002/eap.1667) confirm that *såli* are the main avian visitors of *donne' såli* in the wild, although *canario* (golden white-eye, *Cleptornis marchei*) have also been recorded eating fruits.

*Donne' såli* is a quintessential part of CHamoru cuisine, used in dishes like [*kådun pika*](https://www.annieschamorrokitchen.com/kadun-pika-spicy-chicken/) and most famously, the [*fina'dene'*](https://www.annieschamorrokitchen.com/finadene-chamorro-special-sauce/) sauce that accompanies most savory dishes. While donne' såli can be grown in gardens, in the Marianas *donne' såli* picked from wild-grown plants in the jungle are highly valued as they are perceived to be of higher quality. (Particularly, they are supposedly spicier. Our preliminary research shows that wild-collected donne' såli can be 4 times as spicy as those grown in pots!) The island of Tinian is especially famous for its abundant populations of wild *donne' såli*, and pickers there take care to disperse and maintain wild plants. While *donne' såli* plants were once common in the jungles of Guåhan, [it is incredibly rare to find them in the wild today](https://doi.org/10.1002/eap.1667).

## What are We Doing?
**Donne' Såli Seed Bank @ UMass Amherst** // We are reviving the last-known wild plants found in Guåhan. Thanks to the efforts of many local experts on Guåhan, we have been able to obtain seeds from the last known wild plants seen on the island. We are establishing a seed bank of local *donne' såli* accessions at the University of Massachusetts Amherst, where we are currently rearing plants in the CNS Greenhouse. Our goal is to meticulously hand-pollinate these plants to produce seeds for research and distribution in Guåhan. We expect to see our first fruits by the end of 2026!

**Donne' Såli Spiciness Index** // *Donne' såli* fruit from the wild are prized, as  they are perceived to be spicier than garden-grown fruit. In collaboration with the Horticulture Lab at the University of Guam, we are conducting the first quantitative measurements of the spiciness in wild *donne' såli* from the Marianas. Our preliminary results show that wild-collected *donne'* has an average Scolville Heat Unit of about 280,000. That's close to most grocery store habañeros, and 4 times spicier than wild *donne' såli* seeds grown in pots. Once we complete analysis our greenhouse-grown fruit, we plan to share our results with local newspapers in Guåhan and the Northern Mariana Islands. 

**Native Bird Class** // In collaboration with artist & science teacher Charlie Stowers, we are creating a 45-minute lesson on the difference between migratory and resident birds in the Marianas, explaining how both of those birds can serve as important seed dispersers to native flora (including *donne' såli*!). We aim to run our first classes in classrooms in Guåhan and Tinian in spring 2027. Our curriculum will also be made publicly available at no cost on [BioQuest QUBES](https://qubeshub.org/) as soon as it is complete.

**Tabling at the Donne' Festivals** // We host a table in the annual Donne' Festival held on the islands of Tinian and Guåhan. It was a pleasure meeting everyone at the 2026 event! We plan to table at the 2027 Donni Festival in Tinian and at the 2027 Pika Fest in Guåhan.

## How Can You Help?
As our project takes off, the most valuable thing we'll need is **local knowledge.** Our current estimation of the status of *donne' såli* on Guåhan has been the culmination of months of field work and the intimate knowledge of a panel of field experts who do regular work in the jungles of Guåhan. But even with that, we may have missed something. If you are on Guåhan and have any information about the location of a wild *donne' såli* plant, please email the Project Leader at srclemente(at)tuta(dot)io.
